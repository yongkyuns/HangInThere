# Pull-up grip estimates

Grip policy v2 is optional metadata. iOS retains Apple Vision body inference and its Swift counter; Android retains MediaPipe Heavy and its Kotlin counter. Neither counter consumes hand orientation or width to accept a rep. Shared Kotlin owns wrist crops, geometric association, interpretation, source-time confirmation, bounded timelines, rep summaries, variation totals, and correction policy. Native code owns SDK handles, frames, storage and presentation.

Both platforms use the same checksum-pinned Hand Landmarker full v1 and Pose Landmarker Lite v1 bundles. The secondary Lite model supplies an independent estimated body frame, without changing the primary pose engine. iOS uses the official 0.10.21 binary release because its simulator slice supports Intel and ARM; Android keeps the existing 0.10.32 release. SDK/model IDs are provenance, not accuracy guarantees. Inferred monocular depth is not a measured anatomical coordinate or grip-confidence score.

## Input and geometry

Canonical pixels are upright and unmirrored, including the front camera. Camera-vector axes are right/down/away; hand and body world origins differ. Only vectors within each model result are compared. Missing depth stays unavailable. Native adapters extract six named body points and six hand points; no native policy classifies a grip.

Crop policy v2 first preserves the original forearm-scaled source-pixel recipe: 1.5 times an observed forearm length, bounded to 12–40% of the short image dimension, shifted 15% beyond the wrist; without a usable elbow the first size is 20%. If that crop produces no complete hand landmark result, one wrist-centered crop spanning 28% of the short dimension is tried. Shared Kotlin produces these ordered, clipped, deduplicated candidate lists for both platforms. Each wrist stops at the first complete result, so already detected hands incur no extra model call and each observation returns at most two hands from at most four hand-model calls. Model detection/presence thresholds remain 0.5. The crop version is included in backend provenance.

Crops translate coordinates back to the original image without rotation or reflection. Points outside their actual source crop remain unavailable even if their restored coordinates fall inside the full image. First-pass identity/geometry ambiguity is not a reason to search for a preferred grip label: existing geometric checks and bilateral source-time confirmation still decide whether evidence is usable. The JSON inference bridge also exposes an offline geometric estimate for evaluation; it does not bypass live confirmation. These framing sizes are engineering defaults, not calibrated workout accuracy thresholds.

The independent body detection must provide six strong body landmarks, with valid shoulder/hip geometry and reasonable segment lengths. Its four shoulders-and-wrists anchors must agree with confident primary-body anchors within 12% of the short dimension. Primary hips are additional identity evidence when confident: a conflicting confident hip rejects the frame, while weak or absent primary hips do not erase the strong auxiliary frame. Missing/weak primary shoulders or wrists, weak auxiliary hips, duplicate joints or malformed scores remain unavailable. Grip policy v2 records this distinction; historical policy-v1 records retain their original version and measured labels. Hands associate with anatomical wrists by distance and a separation margin; array order never chooses a side. A confident SDK handedness contradiction is unavailable evidence. Tasks handedness labels are used on canonical unmirrored pixels: an upstream public `right_hands.jpg` smoke inspection yields Right for both independently pictured right hands. Legacy selfie-display assumptions must not introduce another reflection.

The palm normal derives from wrist/index/middle/pinky MCP geometry, with nondegenerate, mutually consistent triangles. Anatomical ordering requires **left −1 / right +1** times `cross(index−wrist, pinky−wrist)`. The literal test layouts are independently defined; the convention was checked against [Gray's Anatomy plate 219, left hand palmar view](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gray219.png). Palm alignment with body facing gives overhand/underhand; inward lateral alignment gives neutral. Intermediate orientations, conflicting identity and unavailable anatomy remain unknown. Bilateral different orientations yield mixed grip. Stability cannot remove hidden-hand or monocular ambiguity.

Width measures wrist separation projected along the acquired bar, divided by a median shoulder-span baseline frozen during an observed ready hang. Baseline acquisition requires five frames spanning at least 0.4 seconds with at most 15% variation. Width requires fresh near-frontal body context (`abs(facing.z) ≥ 0.85`) and current bilateral wrist contact; it does not require a known hand orientation. Narrow `<0.85`, wide `>1.30`, and the intervening shoulder-width band are camera-relative engineering categories, with median filtering/hysteresis. They are not centimeters or a validated population standard. View/setup changes require fresh calibration.

## Time, history and corrections

Secondary inference is disabled for resolved dip sessions and scheduled at most four times per source second for pull-ups, with one task/image in flight. Late completion never blocks body tracking. Results expire after 0.5 seconds, and repeated cached results cannot confirm hands or earn hand-observed time. Three distinct hand observations spanning 0.5 seconds confirm each side; gaps over 0.4 seconds reset that evidence. Setup/interruption/body-loss cutoffs and native generations reject old pending results. The Apple bridge uses the original source-second clock for this metadata, so truncating the geometry transport timestamp cannot turn a pre-loss result into fresh evidence. Blocking SDK calls use the existing isolated native-frame executor on iOS and an owned serial executor on Android.

Independent orientation and width timelines retain at most 128 spans each. Journals merge bounded deltas; model/backend and interruption boundaries remain explicit. Per-attempt summaries need at least 80% credited coverage for each dimension. A supported change inside an attempt is “Grip changed”/“Width changed.” Missing coverage remains unknown. Native review shows completed-rep totals by variation, per-attempt labels, and original bilateral orientation coverage; incomplete attempts never enter variation rep totals. Raw hand points/pixels are not retained in workout history.

Annotations use a separate atomic correction directory. Each versioned event replaces labels for one attempt or the whole finalized set, with an ordered sequence and timestamp. The newest applicable event wins; “Automatic estimate” restores the measured dimension. Measured records, counts, timings, angles and coverage remain immutable. User labels are explicitly marked. Corrections are not inferred evidence and do not retroactively change readiness, rep acceptance, exercise-family goals or coaching metrics. Ledger growth is bounded to 1,024 events per set.

## Validation limits

Common tests cover literal anatomy, identity ambiguity, absent depth, cached/delayed observations, gaps, sustained outages, bounded/deduplicated primary and fallback crops, required upper-body identity vs optional primary hips, historical policy preservation, mixed/changed grips, coverage, corrections, old records and incomplete attempts. Apple checks exercise crop planning/offline estimation and the real JSON/array FFI, correction persistence, separate baseline/grip-enabled 2,000-frame checks against the unchanged 5ms host budget, replay exercise switching, and narrow/wide UI renders. Native iOS and Android smoke tests load both pinned models, run body inference and all primary/fallback hand crops on authored blank pixels, and demand unavailable evidence rather than fabricated grips. Android API29/API35 engineering CI runs these alongside existing native/parity evaluations.

The private chin-up footage remains local. A development crop sweep at unchanged thresholds recovered both hands in all five sampled gripping snapshots using the wrist-centered context crop. A comparison using the actual production Apple body poses improves bilateral hand detection from three of five snapshots to five of five; With grip policy v2, the shared geometric estimate correctly reports underhand in four of five snapshots; the remaining snapshot still lacks the auxiliary body frame and stays unknown. These are development-exposed Python/native-pose diagnostics, not a full-video or held-out grip qualification. Unknown remains the correct output for insufficient bilateral/body evidence. Field accuracy for underhand/overhand/neutral/mixed grips, spacing, occluded/closed hands, front/rear camera and varied viewing angles, plus sustained physical-device inference latency/thermal cost, still need independently labelled capture sets. No rep/form qualification gates are relaxed by this feature.
